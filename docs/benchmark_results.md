## Gate 3: severity and separation

The deterministic campaign comprises five single-defect severities (20--60%)
and fifteen two-defect cases: severities 20%, 40%, and 60%, each at 18, 30, 50,
80, and 120 mm separation. A 10% case was rejected from fitting because the
selected cavity correlation crosses its conduction/convection branch there and
nearly cancels the imposed signal.

The single-defect heat penalty rises from 0.174% at 20% narrowing to 2.098% at
60%. The three analytical coupled-sheet lengths are 19.867, 13.233, and
10.311 mm. The selected empirical single length is 12.391 mm; the two-exponential
fit uses 10.585 and 12.391 mm.

The near-tail calibration errors are small, but blocked extrapolation is not:

| Severity | Single test L2 | Two-exp test L2 | Modal test L2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.2 | 0.642 | 0.589 | 0.680 |
| 0.3 | 0.594 | 0.552 | 0.642 |
| 0.4 | 0.577 | 0.540 | 0.629 |
| 0.5 | 0.579 | 0.541 | 0.631 |
| 0.6 | 0.595 | 0.553 | 0.643 |

The two-exponential form wins all five spatial held-out comparisons, but none
is a reliable global extrapolator. The finite section develops a weak return
field and eventual sign change that positive semi-infinite exponentials cannot
represent.

Pair non-additivity is strong at 18--30 mm and generally falls to order 1e-3 by
50--80 mm. Models calibrated only from a single-defect tail overpredict the
small long-separation interaction, so decay length alone is not yet an accepted
additivity criterion. At held-out separations, the best of the three requested
models depends on severity: single exponential at 0.2 and 0.4, analytical modes
at 0.6. This is a negative but decision-relevant result.

The 30% defect increment is 0.062357, 0.062503, and 0.062510 W m-1 on the
120x36, 160x48, and 240x72 meshes. Medium-to-fine change is 0.0116%; maximum
energy imbalance is 3.46e-11.

## Gates 4 and 5: spatial uncertainty and measured execution paths

The required 10,000-realization campaign was then run with synthetic correlated
gap fields. Fixed topology was verified against the original operator before
timing. CPU process ensembles were measured and rejected on this Windows host;
a reused nominal factorization used as a PCG preconditioner was accepted and
became the float64 production oracle. Batched GPU execution was attempted only
after those measurements.

The CPU campaign completed in 25.79 s at 387.8 cases/s. A CuPy float32 GPU path
completed the same stored 10,000 fields in 4.11 s at 2,435 cases/s, a measured
6.28x speedup. All four declared observables agree with the CPU oracle within
1.31e-4 relative error. Detailed distributions, crossover data, limitations,
and the synthetic-input boundary are reported in `many_query_results.md`.

## Decision

All five milestone gates pass. Retain fixed-CSR topology and nominal-
factorization PCG for the reference path; reject process ensembles for this host
and problem size; retain GPU batches only for large ensembles and the declared
2e-4-tolerance observables. The scientific result is not a tolerance claim for
manufactured IGUs: measured or mechanically generated gap maps are required
before product-level risk statements.
